# Rigged cast, batch 4 (#150), Blender half and the palette design

Batch 4 adds the last two classes, the **bard** and the **artificer** (all 13 classes are then rigged), and designs the **70 subclass palettes** that recolour the class rigs without new meshes or atlases. Everything is original and generated by script in Blender 5.2.3 LTS; no outside content. The skeleton contract is unchanged ([`../r001/README.md`](../r001/README.md): 25 bones, sockets, 30 fps, in place, 1024x1024 atlas of 13 tiles, RGBA vertex colour with the glow mask in alpha, at most 4 influences; these meshes use 3) and the ten shared clips (Brace from batch 2) are used as they are. Earlier notes: [`README.md`](README.md), [`README_BATCH2.md`](README_BATCH2.md), [`README_BATCH3.md`](README_BATCH3.md).

**No batch 1, 2 or 3 file is edited**, because their immutable asset records hash them. Batch 4 sits beside them: `cast_batch4.py` (recipes, atlases; imports `cast_batch3.py`, which imports `cast_batch2.py` for the Brace clip), `build4.py`, `review4.py`, `make_records4.py`, and for the palettes `palette_tiles.py`, `palettes.py`, `palette_preview.py`.

## The 70 subclass palettes

Presentation only (ADR-0001): a palette is chosen from the build id and never fed back into the rules, the snapshot or any random draw.

**Mechanism.** No mesh and no atlas changes. The atlas has 13 tiles on a 4x4 grid (the `REGIONS` order: plate, trim, cloth, leather, blade, glow, rune, skin, bone, accent, dark, emblem, emblem2; tile index = row x 4 + column, row 0 at the top, which is also `floor(v*4)*4 + floor(u*4)` of the UV in Unreal). The master material `M_DiceRollTheme` gets five **slots**; each slot has a bit mask over the 13 tiles (a scalar `Pal<slot>Mask`), a target colour whose alpha is a reference luminance (`Pal<slot>`), and the shader replaces the colour of a pixel in a masked tile by `target x min(luminance(pixel) / reference, 1.7)`. The tile keeps its folds, bands and highlights and takes the new hue. With every mask at 0 (the default of every material instance) the material is exactly what it was. The values are set per figure on its material instance from the build id, so the `MI_SK_*` assets and the class meshes and atlases are untouched.

**Slots.** A primary garment, B secondary garment, C trim, G glow (the spell colour) and X, the **duplicate-identity accent**. Which tiles each slot covers is per class (`palettes.json`, `classes`), designed against the measured tile colours and surface-area shares in `palettes/tiles.json` (`palette_tiles.py`): for example the fighter's A is the steel plate tile, its B the crimson cape and its C the gold trim; the wizard's A is the robe, B the lavender panels.

**The table.** `palettes/palettes.json` is the reviewable data: for each of the 70 catalog builds a `[hue, saturation, luminance ratio]` per slot (a missing slot keeps the class colour), a name and a note. The luminance ratio is relative to the class's own colour of that slot, so 1.0 keeps the class's brightness and the generator fits the hue and saturation to it: a variant cannot silently become darker than its class. `python3 Art/rig-pilot/cast/scripts/palettes.py` resolves it into `palettes.resolved.json` (hex and linear colours, masks, reference luminances), `swatches.html` (the review page) and `Source/DnDDiceRoll/Private/Presentation/ExpeditionPaletteTable.inl` (compiled in); `--check` fails when a committed output is stale (a helper test runs it). A build id with no row falls back to its class default, which is the class's own colours except for the fighter (below); an unknown class gets no palette at all.

**Recognisably the same class.** Silhouette and gear are untouched; only slot colours change, and every variant keeps the class's brightness. `palette_preview.py` previews the recolour in Blender before Unreal (the in-game contact sheet is the evidence).

**Fighter fix.** The fighter's worst shot was 1.38 on the Vault (yaw 90), blue steel on blue stone, under the 1.4 floor. The fighter's class default and `fighter.champion` take a warm silver steel (hue 38, saturation 0.06, luminance x1.12), keeping the crimson cape and gold trim, and the other four fighters take their own steels.

**Duplicate-identity accents.** A class that appears more than once in the party tints its X tiles (the most visible secondary garment: the fighter's cape and shield face, the rogue's sash and hood lining, the cleric's tabard, and so on, 10 to 41% of the surface) with a hue keyed to the party slot (slot 1 red-orange, 2 blue, 3 green, 4 amber), at a fixed luminance, over the subclass palette. The slot is the unit's position among the party in the encounter, so it is stable across Resume and never touches the rules.

## Scripts (repository root, one Blender process at a time, under the machine lock, always with `--python-exit-code 1`)

| Script | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `blender -b --python-exit-code 1 --python Art/rig-pilot/cast/scripts/build4.py [-- bard artificer]` | builds meshes, atlas, weights, ten clips; exports glbs and `*_source.blend` into `Art/<asset-id>/r001/`; writes `metrics/build_*.json` |
| `... review4.py -- validate <key>` | fresh process: re-imports the glbs and writes `metrics/validate_<key>.json` with a `checks` table and `pass`; any failed check exits non-zero (this is review2.py's gate) |
| `... review4.py -- sheet4 bind\|turn\|clips\|stone` | renders `previews/sheet_23..27` (ImageMagick 7) |
| `... palette_tiles.py` | writes `palettes/tiles.json` (the 13 tiles of each of the 13 classes: mean colour, area share, mean height) |
| `python3 Art/rig-pilot/cast/scripts/palettes.py [--check]` | resolves `palettes.json` into the generated outputs |
| `... palette_preview.py -- <class...>` | Blender preview of the recolour per build into `.local/palettes/` |
| `python3 Art/rig-pilot/cast/scripts/make_records4.py` | writes the two `Art/<asset-id>/r001/asset.json` records; **refuses** (exit 1, nothing written) unless every validation JSON passes |

## Known roughness

Clips are shared. The bard's rapier and the artificer's hammer lower to the floor in Downed and Death (no collision awareness), the bard's plume and the lute neck clip the cape or shoulder in Downed and Recover, and the artificer's apron shows its dark back face in a few Walk frames; the cape, apron and peplum are lightly skinned sheets with no cloth simulation. The striped hose and coat come from the shared fold tile. Palette recolours are monochrome per slot (a tile's second hue, such as a cape's pale hem, takes the slot hue at a lighter value).
