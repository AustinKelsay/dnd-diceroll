# The Waystone set piece r001 (issue #250, rescoping #104)

When the fifth fight is won, the Waystone in the Vault apse flares, the camera frames it, the rune seal guarding the artifact shatters onto the dais, and the artifact is lifted before ARTIFACT RECOVERED. This folder holds the art for the seal. Everything is original, generated by the scripts in `scripts/` in Blender 5.2.3 LTS on the Vault kit r002 libraries (`../r002-vault/scripts/r002_lib.py`, `vault_lib.py`, and r001's `tlib.py` and `prims.py`, imported unchanged). There is no outside content and no paid or AI service.

**Identity:** the Vault kit's look. The basalt is the Waystone's blue-black flow stone (`vault_lib.basalt_tile`). The one glow accent is the Vault's cold blue (0.5, 0.85, 1.0), carried only by the vertex-alpha glow mask on the `rune` material.

Conventions are as the Vault kit:
- metres, +X forward, Z up, glTF Y-up export;
- one small atlas per glb (256²), linear and mipmapped;
- `COLOR_0` as `VEC4` normalised uint16, where RGB is self ambient occlusion × a soft height gradient and A is the glow mask.

## Rebuild

From the repository root (a few seconds; under the machine lock on the shared host):

```sh
blender -b --python Art/dark-fantasy-sources/r003-setpiece/scripts/build_setpiece_vault.py   # three glbs + setpiece_metrics.json
python3 Art/dark-fantasy-sources/r003-setpiece/scripts/write_records_setpiece.py              # asset.json for each export
```

The build is byte-deterministic: two runs gave identical glbs (SHA-256).

## Assets (Art/<asset-id>/r001/)

| Asset id | Unreal package | Triangles | Pivot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `setpiece-vault-seal-upper` | `/Game/DnDDiceRoll/Theme/SetPiece/SM_SetPiece_Vault_SealUpper` | 120 | centre of the crack plane; the shard's upper 0.30 m |
| `setpiece-vault-seal-lower` | `.../SetPiece/SM_SetPiece_Vault_SealLower` | 108 | the same point; the lower 0.20 m, tip down |
| `setpiece-vault-chip` | `.../SetPiece/SM_SetPiece_Vault_Chip` | 42 | centre of a 12 cm basalt chip with a rune fleck |

Each shard is a tapered hexagonal basalt crystal with a rune channel on its flat +X face. It is split on one jagged crack: the same crack function is applied to both halves, so they mate exactly. `setpiece_metrics.json` records the crack: 7 shared vertices, 0 m mismatch. Both halves have their pivot on the crack, so the game draws the intact shard by placing both halves at one transform.

All three meshes are under 1k triangles, so they have no LOD exports, as for the Vault scatter. Each is imported with its own `MI_*` of `M_DiceRollTheme` (Glow 0.45, roughness 0.85).

## How the game uses them (no physics)

Six shards hang in a ring of 75 cm radius around the artifact. They lean 12° outward, with the rune faces turned out. The flight plan is pre-authored in `Source/DnDDiceRoll/Private/Presentation/WaystoneSetPiece.cpp` (`WaystoneSetPiece::Plan`). Each half and each of 12 chips has:
- a fixed resting spot on the dais, given against the apse geometry of `build_vault_r002.py` (`waystone_local`): steps 20, 40 and 60 cm high, starting 105, 65 and 25 cm in front of the pedestal;
- an arc;
- whole tumbling turns;
- a small hop.

The pieces land clear of:
- the monolith, 150 cm behind;
- the kneeling statues, 190 cm to each side;
- the brazier ring: nothing lands nearer the board than 150 cm.

On the primitive look, the same plan is drawn in to 80 % around the legacy corner pedestal, with engine cubes as the pieces.

## Known limits

- The Blender scripts produce no previews of the set piece in motion. The arrangement and the motion exist only in game, and the evidence is the in-game frame burst (`tools/capture/setpiece-poses.json`; see #250's PR).
- The halves lie on their sides at a fixed 8 cm above the floor. A half that rests on a hexagon vertex rather than a face can sink or float by up to about 0.6 cm.
